English minimal pairs for stress

By Tom McCoy

Here is a list of pairs of English words that differ only in where stress falls in them:

  1. desert / dessert
  2. insight / incite
  3. reflex / reflects
  4. discus / discuss
  5. readout / redoubt
  6. misery / Missouri
  7. deepened / depend
  8. media / Medea
  9. uprise / apprise
  10. abbess / abyss
  11. bellies / Belize
  12. expos / expose
  13. trusty / trustee
  14. conquer / concur

This list is not complete, and you might disagree about some of the examples since you might pronounce some of the words differently from how I do. I made this list by searching over words in the CMU pronouncing dictionary and then filtering for the pairs that I liked best. Note that English has some systematic pairings of this sort where a noun and a verb that have related meanings differ only in their stress, such as the noun *REcord* (as in a record player) vs. the verb *reCORD* (as in to record a movie), the noun *PROduce* (as in what a grocery store sells) vs. the verb *proDUCE* (meaning to make), and the noun *CONduct* (as in a code of conduct) vs. the verb *conDUCT* (as in to conduct an orchestra). Other examples are *PREsent*/*preSENT*, *SUspect*/*suSPECT*, *CONflict*/*conFLICT*, *PROgress*/*proGRESS*, and *INsult*/*inSULT*. However, I restricted the list above to only include pairings that did not arise from this systematic process. One interesting case is the noun *ENtrance* (meaning an entryway) vs. the verb *enTRANCE* (meaning to fascinate); these might look like another example of systematic noun/verb correspondences, but in fact the two words are unrelated (*ENtrance* has a root relating to *entry*, while *enTRNACE* has a root relating to *trance*).

There are also some examples that are unidirectional (e.g., changing the stress of *Javanese* can yield *Giovanni's*, but changing the stress of *Giovanni's* would not yield *Javanese*):

  1. Javanese→Giovanni's
  2. Plato→plateau
  3. aerobic→Arabic
  4. cereal→surreal
  5. bellow→below
  6. decade→decayed
  7. arrows→arose

In 2019, I published a New York Times crossword based on examples like these.
