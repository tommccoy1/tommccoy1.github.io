Duality Quality

By Tom McCoy

It is sometimes possible to make a small change to a word's spelling that yields a large change in its pronunciation. This page focuses on one such situation: cases where there are two English words whose spelling differs by only one letter but which have different numbers of syllables. Here is a list of some such pairs that I particularly like:

  1. doable / double
  2. duality / quality
  3. oasis / basis
  4. promoting / prompting
  5. nicety / ninety
  6. recipe / recite
  7. pilates / pirates
  8. naive / waive
  9. vaseline / baseline
  10. protean / protein
  11. realty / really
  12. delirious / delicious
  13. heresy / hereby
  14. aloha / alpha
  15. uranium / cranium
  16. value / vague
  17. avenue / avenge
  18. bridle / bridge
  19. cameo / camel
  20. previous / precious
  21. druid / droid
  22. reverie / reverse
  23. hideous / hideout

In all of the above cases, the syllable counts of the two words differ by one. There are also some examples that change the syllable count by two:

  1. fiance / France
  2. created / creaked

Sometimes a single spelling can have multiple pronunciations that differ in their syllable count. Some of these are simply due to variation across people in how they pronounce words (e.g., some people say caramel with two syllables while others say it with three), and others are due to the fact that certain words permit a vowel sound to be dropped depending on how much you want to enunciate (e.g., the middle syllable can optionally be dropped in cabinet, coverage, and probably). My favorite examples are words that can be analyzed as having two different structures that yield different pronunciations. An honorable mention for this category is twinkling, which I believe tends to be pronounced with two syllables when it means "a brief moment" (as in "in a twinkling") but three syllables when it means "shining" (as in "the stars are twinkling"). However, that one is not clear-cut because I think that either pronunciation could be used for either meaning (I think there are trends favoring one pronunciation for one meaning and the other pronunciation for the other meaning, but I don't think these differences are absoluate). Here are two examples that give a clearer difference:

  1. reaching
    1. 2 syllables: "arriving at" (i.e., "reach" + "-ing")
    2. 3 syllables: "aching again" (i.e., "re-" + "aching")
  2. unionized
    1. 3 syllables: "formed a union" (i.e., "unionize" + "-ed")
    2. 4 syllables: "not ionized" (i.e., "un-" + "ionized")

The title for this page (Duality Quality) comes from an excellent 2017 New York Times crossword by Natan Last, Finn Vigeland and the J.A.S.A. Crossword Class.
